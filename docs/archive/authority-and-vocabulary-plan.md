# Authority and controlled vocabulary plan

## Purpose

Controlled terms improve consistency and exchange without erasing the language, uncertainty, or contested identities present in the historical record. The archive preserves source wording separately from normalized access points.

## Priority authorities

- People and organizations: local authority records first, with VIAF, ISNI, ORCID, Wikidata, or another reviewed URI when appropriate.
- Places: GeoNames, Wikidata, or an authoritative local geographic source, while retaining historical names and boundary uncertainty.
- Subjects and genres: Library of Congress authorities, Getty vocabularies, UNESCO vocabularies, or a documented local vocabulary selected for the collection and language.
- Audiovisual roles, asset types, physical carriers, and generations: PBCore controlled vocabularies.
- Preservation events and rights bases: Library of Congress preservation vocabularies associated with PREMIS.
- Rights: exact Creative Commons or RightsStatements.org URIs after review.
- Languages: BCP 47 language tags, with the spoken or written variety retained when known.
- File formats: PRONOM PUIDs and Library of Congress format descriptions where identified.

## Local authority records

Every local agent or subject receives a stable La Pipa ID, preferred label, alternative labels, type, source, review state, and optional external URI. Two names are not merged solely because strings match. A person’s current preferred name and historically attested names may coexist with context and access controls.

## Change control

Vocabulary additions are additive and reviewed. Deprecated terms remain resolvable and point to their successor. Changes record date, reason, evidence, editor, and affected records. Automated entity resolution proposes matches; a reviewer approves merges and split decisions.

## Multilingual description

Spanish, Asturian, and English descriptions are independently curated where the audience requires them. Translation is labeled and linked to its source. Proper names, culturally specific terms, and historical language are not flattened for convenience.

## Ethical guardrail

Authority control is not authority over people. The archive documents why a label is used, accepts correction and preferred-name requests, restricts harmful access terms where appropriate, and preserves necessary historical evidence without promoting harmful language as the preferred description.
