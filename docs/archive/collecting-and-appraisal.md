# Collecting and appraisal policy

## Collecting scope

The archive collects materials with a demonstrable relationship to La Pipa, including:

- evidence of formation, identity, governance, locations, and chronology;
- photographs, posters, flyers, tickets, publications, correspondence, plans, and administrative records;
- audio, video, film, oral histories, performances, rehearsals, interviews, and field recordings;
- artworks, designs, installations, editions, digital works, websites, and social-media captures;
- documentation of participants, collaborators, audiences, neighbors, partners, and critics;
- contracts, licenses, permissions, consent records, and rights research;
- press, scholarship, reviews, ephemera, and third-party documentation;
- contextual records needed to interpret the collection responsibly;
- born-digital project files and production documentation for new La Pipa documentaries.

## Appraisal criteria

Each offer or discovery is assessed for provenance, relevance, uniqueness, evidential value, informational value, authenticity, condition, technical risk, rights, sensitivity, duplication, cost of stewardship, and relationship to existing holdings. Scarcity and fame are not the only measures: everyday operational traces and marginalized perspectives may carry exceptional documentary value.

## Appraisal decisions

Allowed decisions are accept, accept selectively, defer pending research, refer to a more appropriate repository, or decline. The decision, rationale, appraiser, date, offeror, restrictions, and disposal or return action must be recorded. Declined material is not copied or retained without authority.

## Acquisition and custody

No material becomes a managed accession until the archive records the source, transfer basis, date received, extent, restrictions, and custody evidence. Physical custody, intellectual property, privacy rights, and permission to publish are separate questions and must never be conflated.

## Arrangement

Respect original order when it is meaningful and document any imposed arrangement. The default hierarchy is fonds or collection, series, subseries, file, item. Item-level description is prioritized for audiovisual works, unique digital objects, rights-sensitive materials, frequently requested items, and documentary-production selects.

## Deaccessioning

Deaccessioning requires owner approval, a recorded rationale, rights review, provenance review, and a disposition event. Database tombstones and audit history remain even when content must be destroyed or returned. Destruction must be authorized, scoped, witnessed where appropriate, and recorded as irreversible.
