# Rights, ethics, privacy, and access policy

## Access rule

The most open defensible access is preferred, but no object is public by default. Access decisions combine copyright, contract, consent, privacy, data protection, cultural sensitivity, safety, donor terms, condition, and preservation risk. Possession of a copy is not permission to publish it.

## Access tiers

- **Closed:** no user access; preservation and authorized legal or curatorial work only.
- **Restricted:** named staff or approved users under recorded conditions.
- **Reading room:** mediated research access with copying or redistribution controls.
- **Public:** approved for public delivery with the recorded credit and use conditions.

## Review questions

Before publication, establish who created and contributed to the work; who owns relevant rights; whether rights were transferred or licensed; what consent covers; whether people are identifiable; whether the material reveals sensitive personal data, minors, health, sexuality, legal matters, addresses, security details, or confidential communications; whether disclosure may create present harm; whether an embargo or takedown route is required; and what credit or contextual statement must travel with the object.

## Evidence and uncertainty

Rights conclusions cite their evidence source. `Unknown` is an acceptable basis when research is incomplete, but it cannot support unrestricted public release. RightsStatements.org and Creative Commons URIs are used only after review; they are not decorative labels.

## Ethical description

Historical language may be retained when necessary as quoted evidence while current descriptive metadata uses respectful terms and explains harmful or contested terminology. Do not erase the historical record, but do not reproduce harm without purpose and context. People represented in the collection should have a documented route to supply corrections, context, preferred names, or access concerns.

## Redaction and withdrawal

Redaction creates a new representation and preservation event; the restricted original remains intact when lawful and ethical to retain. Withdrawal suppresses access while retaining the stable identifier, reason, authorization, and audit history. Emergency takedown requests are logged, acknowledged, risk-assessed promptly, and reviewed by an authorized decision-maker.

## RAG safeguards

The answer layer must enforce access before retrieval, not after generation. Citations include stable source IDs and verification status. Sensitive text must not enter prompts, logs, analytics, or embeddings outside its permitted processing boundary. Answers distinguish documented fact, attributed recollection, interpretation, inference, contradiction, and unresolved uncertainty.
